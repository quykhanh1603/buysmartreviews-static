Vroom was an online used-car retailer that let buyers purchase vehicles sight-unseen for home delivery. Here’s an honest look based on public review data.

Quick verdict

Vroom’s review record is considerably more negative than most companies we’ve covered, with a consistent pattern of complaints across platforms. Note that Vroom wound down its car-selling operations in 2024, so this review is primarily useful for buyers researching past experiences or checking on an existing purchase.

What customer ratings show

  • Trustpilot: reviews skew heavily negative.
  • BBB: profile status and rating vary by source; some listings note the business as no longer operating in its original form.

Recurring themes

Negative: the most serious recurring complaints involve vehicles arriving with undisclosed damage, disputes over warranty coverage, and long delays — in some cases reported as over four months — for registration and title paperwork.

What to check

  • If you have an existing Vroom purchase with an outstanding title/warranty issue, document everything and check your state attorney general’s consumer complaint process if the company itself isn’t responsive.
  • For future used-car purchases generally, get an independent, in-person or third-party inspection before finalizing, especially for sight-unseen online purchases.

Bottom line

Vroom’s public review record shows a heavier concentration of serious complaints than most companies in this category, and its car-selling business has since wound down. Treat this one with more caution than most.

Ratings referenced above reflect publicly available data at the time of writing and may have changed since publication.
