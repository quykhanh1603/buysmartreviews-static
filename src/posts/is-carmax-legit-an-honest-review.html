CarMax is one of the largest used-car retailers in the US, with hundreds of locations nationwide. Here’s an honest look based on public review data.

Quick verdict

CarMax is a large, legitimate, publicly traded company — not a scam. But there’s a striking gap between its BBB rating and its Trustpilot record, worth understanding before you buy or sell.

What customer ratings show

  • BBB: A+ rating.
  • Trustpilot: around 1.8 out of 5, with the BBB profile itself showing well over 2,000 complaints in the past three years.

An A+ BBB grade mainly reflects whether a company responds to formal complaints filed through the BBB, not overall customer satisfaction — which is why the Trustpilot number tells a very different story here.

Recurring themes

Negative: the clearest complaint pattern involves issues discovered after purchase, including undisclosed prior accident history on some vehicles, and difficulty reaching or getting resolution from customer service.

Positive: CarMax’s no-haggle pricing and structured return policy are cited favorably by buyers who didn’t run into a post-purchase issue.

Before you buy or sell

  • Run an independent vehicle history report (Carfax/AutoCheck) yourself rather than relying solely on CarMax’s disclosures.
  • Get a pre-purchase inspection from your own trusted mechanic if the return window allows it.
  • Keep all paperwork from the sale in case you need to escalate an issue.

Bottom line

CarMax is a legitimate, major used-car retailer, but its Trustpilot record suggests inconsistent experiences with post-purchase issues. Independent verification of vehicle history is worth the extra step.

Ratings referenced above reflect publicly available data at the time of writing and may have changed since publication.
