Blue Nile is one of the oldest and best-known online jewelers in the US, specializing in certified diamonds and custom engagement rings. Here’s what the publicly available data says about buying from them.

Quick verdict

Blue Nile is a well-established, legitimate jeweler with strong quality controls (certified stones, no-commission sales staff). The main thing to watch is turnaround time on repairs and custom orders.

What customer ratings show

  • Trustpilot: rated around 4 out of 5 across more than 1,500 reviews.
  • Headquartered in Seattle, WA; all diamonds sold are independently certified.

Recurring themes

Positive: reviewers consistently mention that items arrive matching their online description, live-chat support is genuinely helpful for sizing questions, and the 30-day return policy is honored without hassle. Sales staff work on salary rather than commission, which several customers cite as a reason they felt no pressure to upsell.

Negative: the most common complaint is turnaround time — some customers reported jewelry repairs taking up to several months, and a smaller number mentioned delayed orders around important dates like anniversaries or proposals.

Before you order

  • If you need a ring by a specific date (e.g., a proposal), order with significant buffer time and confirm the estimated ship date in writing.
  • Keep the independent grading certificate that comes with certified stones — useful for insurance and resale.
  • For repairs, ask for a time estimate upfront and follow up proactively rather than waiting to hear back.

Bottom line

Blue Nile is a legitimate, long-standing retailer well-suited for buyers who plan ahead. Time-sensitive purchases (like a proposal on a fixed date) deserve extra buffer given reported delays on repairs and some orders.
