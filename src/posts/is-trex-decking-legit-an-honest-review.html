Trex is the largest composite decking manufacturer in the US, widely used for outdoor decks and railing. Here’s an honest look based on public review data — including a notable contrast between industry recognition and consumer complaints.

Quick verdict

Trex is a legitimate, well-established, industry-leading manufacturer, but there’s a real gap between its marketed “most trusted” industry awards and specific complaints from individual homeowners — worth understanding both sides.

What customer ratings show

  • Industry recognition: named “America’s Most Trusted Outdoor Decking” by Lifestory Research for multiple consecutive years, based on aggregate brand trust survey data.
  • PissedConsumer: around 1.7 out of 5 from 75 reviews (note: this platform specifically attracts dissatisfied customers, so treat it as a complaint log rather than a representative average).
  • BBB: not accredited, with complaints on file.

Recurring themes

Negative: warping fascia boards and warranty claims being denied are the most specific, recurring complaints — including from customers who used a professional, certified contractor for installation.

Before you order

  • Read Trex’s warranty terms in detail, particularly what voids coverage (certain fastening methods, gap spacing, ventilation requirements).
  • Use a Trex-certified installer and keep all installation documentation, since warranty disputes appear to hinge on installation compliance.

Bottom line

Trex is the market leader with strong industry recognition, but individual warranty claim disputes are a real, documented pattern. Careful installation documentation is worth the effort given the warranty terms.

Ratings referenced above reflect publicly available data at the time of writing and may have changed since publication.
