Dell is one of the largest computer manufacturers in the world, selling laptops, desktops, and enterprise equipment direct to consumers. Here’s an honest look based on public review data.

Quick verdict

Dell is a massive, legitimate, publicly traded company, but there’s a stark gap between its BBB accreditation and actual customer sentiment on independent platforms — among the more pronounced gaps in this category.

What customer ratings show

  • BBB: A+ rating, accredited (note: BBB’s letter grade doesn’t factor in customer review scores).
  • Trustpilot: nearly 3,500 reviews, skewing negative.

Recurring themes

Negative: customer service described as unhelpful or hard to resolve issues through, long support delays, and product/durability complaints including devices failing after relatively short use.

Before you order

  • Consider purchasing an extended warranty/support plan if you anticipate needing hands-on troubleshooting help, given the volume of support-related complaints.
  • Register your product and keep purchase documentation organized in case you need warranty service.

Bottom line

Dell is a legitimate, massive manufacturer, but its BBB accreditation doesn’t reflect actual customer service sentiment. Budget extra patience (or a support plan) if you order direct.

Ratings referenced above reflect publicly available data at the time of writing and may have changed since publication.
